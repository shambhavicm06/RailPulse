The NetworkX engine computes these per-station metrics once and flattens them into tabular columns for the boosting models. Betweenness highlights the network's choke points.

How the flattening works. Degree, eigenvector centrality, PageRank and betweenness summarise each station's position in the 2-D network as 1-D numbers. Together with the dynamic congestion rings (the live delay of all trains within 1/2/3 edges), these columns give classical gradient boosting "network-awareness" — at a fraction of a GNN's compute, and deployable on legacy dispatcher hardware.

Project 17 — Graph-Derived Feature Boosting

The problem. On the saturated Indian Railways network, a 40-minute delay at a junction monopolises track clearance and forces secondary trains to halt — a cascading ripple effect. Conventional models predict each train in isolation.

The idea. Use Network Mathematics (eigenvector centrality, PageRank, betweenness) to flatten the 2-D rail graph into 1-D tabular columns, then feed them to frugal, explainable gradient-boosting ensembles — bypassing GNNs entirely.

The proof. Two ablations: (1) isolated vs graph-aware features (−46% MAE), and (2) the radius-of-influence sweep showing the delay ripple decays with distance.
